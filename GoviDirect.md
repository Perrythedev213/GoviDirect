# SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM

## The Problem: Farmers & Bargaining Power

### Traditional Agricultural Supply Chain

**Farmer → Middleman / Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer**

Create this as a large horizontal visual flow across the slide.

### The Issue

Farmers are part of Sri Lanka's **primary sector**, but many depend on intermediaries and larger businesses to reach consumers.

This can limit farmers' **bargaining power** when negotiating with larger buyers, potentially reducing the price they receive for their produce and affecting their income and standard of living.

### Sri Lankan Farmer Income

Display these as two large statistic cards:

**Rs. 54,430**
*Average monthly income of the principal farmer*

**23%**
*of principal farmers earned below Rs. 25,000 per month*

### Visuals

Use authentic photographs of **Sri Lankan farmers working in agricultural fields**.

### Key Terms

**Primary sector · Bargaining power · Income · Standard of living · Intermediaries · Stakeholders**

### Layout

* **Left:** Supply-chain diagram
* **Right:** Farmer photographs + statistics
* Use large numbers rather than paragraphs.

# SLIDE 3 — THE PRICE GAP

## Where Does the Value Go?

### Bitter Gourd — HARTI Study

| Price                             |           Amount |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------: |
| Farmer / Producer Price           | **Rs. 25.50/kg** |
| Average Colombo Supermarket Price | **Rs. 82.62/kg** |
| Price Difference                  |  **≈ Rs. 57/kg** |

### Key Finding

The average supermarket retail price was approximately **3.24× the price received by conventional farmers**.

### Main Visual

Create a large **two-bar comparison chart**:

**Farmer**
Rs. 25.50/kg

**Supermarket**
Rs. 82.62/kg

Add a large visual callout:

### **3.24×**

*Approximate retail-to-farmer price ratio*

### Explanation

The example illustrates how prices can increase throughout the agricultural **value chain** as produce moves through different market participants.

However, the retail price is **not entirely supermarket profit**. Transportation, storage, labour, operating costs and other expenses contribute to the final consumer price.

### Key Terms

**Producer price · Retail price · Value chain · Price differential · Revenue · Intermediaries · Market structure**

### Design Goal

Make the **Rs. 25.50 → Rs. 82.62** difference the dominant visual element.

# SLIDE 4 — REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

## A Real-World Price Gap

### Carrots — September 2026

Use a large price comparison graphic:

| Market                         |      Approx. Price |
| ------------------------------ | -----------------: |
| Dambulla Wholesale Market      |     **Rs. 185/kg** |
| Keells / Cargills Supermarkets | **Rs. 270–290/kg** |

### Price Difference

At **Rs. 290/kg**:

**≈ 56.76% higher**

than the Dambulla wholesale-market price of Rs. 185/kg.

### Visual

Create a large two-bar chart:

**Dambulla Wholesale**
`Rs. 185/kg`

**Supermarket**
`Rs. 290/kg`

Use a highlighted arrow between the bars showing the increase.

### Important Context

The Dambulla figure is a **wholesale-market price**, not a confirmed farm-gate price.

Therefore, the actual difference between what a farmer receives and what a consumer pays **may be larger**.

Retailers also incur legitimate costs including:

* Transportation
* Storage
* Labour
* Store operations
* Packaging
* Distribution

# SLIDE 5 — OUR SOLUTION

## Our Solution — GoviDirect

### Value Proposition

> **Connect farmers directly with consumers while maintaining trust, quality control and a sustainable revenue model.**

Present the four features as **four large visual cards**.

---

### 🚚 01 — Direct Farm Dispatch

A standard formula calculates transparent dispatch costs so both customers and farmers understand delivery expenses.

---

### 💰 02 — Only 4% Platform Revenue

GoviDirect retains **4% of the product transaction value** as platform revenue.

**96% of product-sale revenue goes to the farmer.**

---

### ✓ 03 — Verified & Reviewed

Farmers can apply for verification by submitting a sample for quality evaluation.

Customers can see:

* Verification status
* Seller reviews
* Product information

---

### 🛡 04 — Quality Accountability

Farmers agree to platform rules and accurately represent product quality.

Misleading listings can result in **platform penalties or legal action**, subject to applicable rules.

### Key Terms

**Value proposition · Revenue model · Quality control · Customer satisfaction · Trust · Stakeholders**

### Layout

Use a **2 × 2 card layout** with icons and very little body text.

# SLIDE 6 — WHY GOVI DIRECT?

## Why GoviDirect?

### Traditional Model

**Farmer → Middlemen → Supermarket → Consumer**

### GoviDirect Model

**Farmer → GoviDirect Platform → Consumer**

Make the GoviDirect model visually shorter and simpler to communicate **disintermediation**.

---

## Three Stakeholders

### 👨‍🌾 Farmers

* Greater direct access to customers
* Greater control over pricing
* Greater share of product-sale revenue

### 🛒 Consumers

* Potentially fairer prices
* Greater price transparency
* Access to fresh produce
* Ability to review sellers

### 🌱 GoviDirect

* Generates revenue through a **4% transaction fee**
* Uses revenue to operate and maintain the platform

---

## Economics & Accounting

### Disintermediation

GoviDirect reduces unnecessary intermediaries between **producer and consumer**.

### Allocative Efficiency

A more direct supply chain can potentially improve how resources and produce are allocated.

### Operating Costs

GoviDirect must still cover costs such as:

**Technology · Staff · Verification · Payment processing · Customer support · Administration**

# SLIDE 7 — HOW GOVI DIRECT WORKS

## How GoviDirect Works

Create a large horizontal **6-step process flow**:

**1. Listing → 2. Verification → 3. Order → 4. Payment → 5. Delivery → 6. Review**

---

### 01 — Farmer Creates a Listing

The farmer enters:

* Produce
* Price
* Quantity
* Quality information

---

### 02 — Optional Verification

The farmer submits a sample for quality evaluation and can receive **Verified** status.

---

### 03 — Customer Orders

The customer:

* Browses listings
* Views seller reviews
* Checks verification status
* Adds products to cart
* Checks out

---

### 04 — Payment

The customer pays through the platform.

GoviDirect processes the transaction and allocates:

**96% → Farmer**
**4% → GoviDirect**

---

### 05 — Direct Delivery

The farmer and customer agree on the transport method and the produce is dispatched directly.

---

### 06 — Review & Tip

After receiving the produce, the customer can:

* Rate the farmer
* Leave feedback
* Give a voluntary tip

**100% of tips go to the farmer.**

### Key Terms

**Transaction · Revenue · Operating costs · Cash flow · Customer feedback**

### Design

Use **icons + arrows**, not six large paragraphs.

# SLIDE 8 — THE DREAM FOR GOVI DIRECT

## The Dream for GoviDirect

Present the five objectives as a **long-term growth roadmap**.

---

### 🏢 01 — Regional Warehouses

As transaction volume increases, GoviDirect could establish regional warehouses for storage and maintenance.

Bulk transportation can create **economies of scale**, reducing average distribution costs per unit while placing produce closer to consumers.

---

### 🌾 02 — Support the Primary Sector

Improve living standards and economic opportunities within Sri Lanka's agricultural and primary industries.

---

### ♻️ 03 — Fresh Food, Less Waste

Promote access to fresh, minimally processed produce while reducing food waste.

Surplus produce approaching expiry could be **donated where practical**.

---

### 📱 04 — Reach Farmers Without Smartphones

## **THE BIGGEST EXPANSION OPPORTUNITY**

JFA data states that only **36% of farmers with phones have smartphone access**, while **61% use basic mobile devices**.

GoviDirect could allow farmers to list products using:

**☎ Calls + SMS**

Staff could manage:

**Listings → Orders → Payments**

This could:

* Expand the supplier base
* Bring more farmers onto the platform
* Increase market reach

But it would also increase:

**Staffing + Administration + Operating Costs**

---

### 🌱 05 — Our Biggest Dream

> **Create a future where farmers receive the value their hard work deserves.**

### Key Terms

**Economies of scale · Sustainability · Waste reduction · Primary sector · Social impact · Long-term growth**

### Design

Make **Objective 04 the largest section** because it represents a major potential expansion of the platform.
